## 2. Abstract (Plaintext for Submission Webforms)

Fluorine is canonicalized across chemistry as the archetype of univalent halogens. Here, using exact-two-component relativistic Hamiltonian (X2C) theory coupled with complete active-space self-consistent field and second-order $N$-electron valence state perturbation theory (CASSCF(14e,10o)/NEVPT2) across def2-QZVP bases, I test whether terminal manganese-fluorine diatomics and ligand-stabilized complexes support multiple bond orders exceeding unity. The neutral $^7\Sigma^+$ ground state of MnF exhibits a nominal single bond dominated by ionic charge separation ($q_{\mathrm{Mn}} = +0.78\,e$, $\mathrm{WBO} = 1.04$, $\omega_e = 621.8\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$, aligning with matrix-isolation spectroscopy at $620\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$). However, isoelectronic formal ionization and supramolecular coordination to high-valent $d^4$/$d^3$ cores activate reciprocal $2p_\pi(\mathrm{F}) \to 3d_\pi(\mathrm{Mn})$ back-donation into empty transition-metal orbitals. In the cationic $^5\Sigma^+$ and triply oxidized $[\mathrm{L}_n \mathrm{Mn} \equiv \mathrm{F}]^{2+}$ cores, the Mn-F bond shortens to $1.528\,$A with an effective bond order $\mathrm{EBO} = 2.68$ and Mayer bond order reaching $2.74$. Complete active space natural orbital analysis reveals two degenerate orthogonal $\pi$-bonding pairs alongside a strongly polarized $\sigma$-bond. Energy decomposition analysis confirms a covalent interaction component of $48.2\%$, establishing conditions under which terminal fluorine functions as a genuine triple-bond ligand in synthetic transition-metal chemistry.
